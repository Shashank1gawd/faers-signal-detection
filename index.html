FAERS = FDA Adverse Event Reporting System — the FDA's public database of adverse event and medication error reports from manufacturers, healthcare professionals and patients.
Educational prototype: not for clinical, regulatory, or patient-care decisions. Verify all imported and inferred information against the original source before use. The 2×2 table

N = a+b+c+d. Expected Ea = (a+b)(a+c)/N — what you'd see if drug and event were unrelated. Every method compares a to Ea.

Disproportionality formulas

PRR (Proportional Reporting Ratio):

PRR = [a/(a+b)] ÷ [c/(c+d)]

ROR (Reporting Odds Ratio):

ROR = (a×d) ÷ (b×c)

95% CI: estimate × exp(±1.96×SE); if the lower bound stays above 1 (or above 0 for IC, log scale), it's unlikely to be pure chance.

χ² (Pearson chi-square for the 2×2 table, 1 degree of freedom, no continuity correction):

χ² = N(ad − bc)² ÷ [(a+b)(c+d)(a+c)(b+d)]

IC (Information Component) — WHO-UMC's Bayesian method (BCPNN), on a log2 scale:

IC~ = log2[(a+0.5) ÷ (Ea+0.5)]

EBGM (Empirical Bayes Geometric Mean) — the FDA's Bayesian method:

EBGM~ = (a+0.5) ÷ (Ea+0.5)

IC and EBGM shown here are simplified point estimates for teaching. The real IC (BCPNN) and EBGM (MGPS) are fitted across the entire database at once to set their shrinkage priors — they can't come from one 2×2 table alone. Real thresholds: EB05 (lower 90% bound) ≥ 2, IC025 (lower 95% bound) > 0. Here, IC025 uses the published Norén approximation IC − 3.3(a+0.5)−½ − 2(a+0.5)−3/2, and the EBGM~ check uses the point estimate ≥ 2 (looser than the real EB05 rule).

Signal rule used here (Evans criteria)

a ≥ 3  and  PRR ≥ 2  and  χ² ≥ 4

Method agreement

Because PRR, ROR, IC~ and EBGM~ can disagree on borderline cases, the app also shows how many of the four independently exceed their own rule-of-thumb threshold (e.g. "3/4 agree"). More agreement is more reassuring than any single number — a common real-world PV practice.

Worked example

a=20, b=80, c=30, d=370 → Ea=10 → PRR 2.67, ROR 3.08, χ² 13.9, IC~ 0.97, EBGM~ 1.95 → clears the Evans rule.

Case-level investigation (Investigate tab)

Pulls a sample (up to 100, most recent first) of the actual reports behind a drug–event pair and breaks them down:

• Serious vs non-serious — FAERS marks a report "serious" if it involved death, a life-threatening event, hospitalization, disability, a congenital anomaly, or was "other medically important".
• Outcome — recovered, recovering, not recovered, recovered with sequelae, fatal, or unknown.
• Age group / sex distribution — from the reporter-supplied demographics.
• Reporting-year trend — counted across the fetched sample only (not the full population), clearly labeled as such.
• Indication — why the drug was prescribed, from the suspect-drug record.
• Concomitant drugs — other medicines listed on the same report (characterized as "concomitant" or "interacting", not "suspect").
• Dechallenge / rechallenge — what happened when the drug was stopped/reduced (dechallenge) and whether the reaction reappeared if the drug was restarted (rechallenge) — strong causality clues when documented.
• Fatal-case review — isolates reports with a fatal outcome for closer reading.

This is a sample, not the full population (the API caps non-aggregate queries at 100 records), and it's PT-level, not full MedDRA SOC/PT hierarchy — the SOC groupings require a licensed MedDRA dictionary this public API doesn't expose.

Reviewer notes & saved searches

Expanding a result row lets you mark it Not reviewed / Confirmed / Dismissed and leave a note — saved locally in your browser (not shared or uploaded anywhere), a lightweight stand-in for a real reviewer-workflow and audit trail. Recently searched drugs are saved the same way, as quick-access chips.

Limitations

• FAERS can't give true incidence rates. • One report can list several drugs or reactions, so counts overlap. • The case sample is capped at 100 and sorted by most recent, not randomly. • Duplicates, media attention and confounding by indication can create false signals. • Drug names vary — try brand and generic spellings. • Every screen needs clinical and PV expert review.

Not included, on purpose

• AI case summarization / narrative generation — would need a live call to an AI model from this page; once published, a Claude artifact page can no longer call Claude's API (that only works in the private editing preview), so it's left out rather than shipped broken.
• Full MedDRA SOC hierarchy — MedDRA is licensed terminology; openFDA exposes Preferred Terms only.
• Literature integration, formal audit/compliance sign-off workflow — these need a real backend and organizational access controls, out of scope for a single static portfolio page.
